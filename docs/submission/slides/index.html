The problem

  • Corn Belt farmers mostly grow corn and soybean in turn. The soil lies bare much of the winter.
  • Adding a cover crop, a small grain or alfalfa is a choice that is hard to undo: it touches soil, water and next year’s harvest.
  • The weather that decides the outcome is uncertain. A single forecast number would be false confidence.
  • NASA satellites see bare soil, drought and soil moisture — but the data sit in many archives a farmer has no time to learn.

What farmers need

Not an AI that picks the answer, but a tool that:

  • compares rotations side by side
  • shows trade-offs and the bad-year tail
  • says what it does not know
  • leaves the decision to the farmer and a local adviser

How it works — two tiers

Tier 1

Quick Screen — anywhere in the Corn Belt

  • Click 4 corners of a field on a satellite map
  • 10 years of NASA POWER weather + USGS 3DEP elevation
  • FAO-56 crop water formulas (ported from our team’s MATLAB prototype V8)
  • Pick 2–3 of corn / soybean / wheat; no ranking
  • Every result labelled “screening estimate”
Tier 2

Deep Analysis — 20 pilot fields, 4 counties

  • Story IA (12), McLean IL (3), Lancaster NE (3), Union OH (2)
  • Field soil (SSURGO), crop history (USDA CDL), field ET (OpenET)
  • Monte Carlo water balance, 500 seasons, fixed seed → P10–P90 + CVaR
  • 4 strategy cards A–D, 2012 drought replay, Prithvi crop check
  • LLM explains only numbers the engine returned

A = current corn–soybean · B = A + cereal rye cover · C = corn–soybean–small grain + cover · D = corn–soybean–alfalfa (reference card). Outside pilot fields: “Deep analysis available in 4 pilot counties.”

Honest by design — and our limits

How we stay honest

  • Future shown only as P10–P90 ranges; tested against the real 2012 drought
  • Numbers never generated by the LLM — each one traced to engine JSON
  • Every card: why, evidence, benefits, information gaps, local-review risks
  • Cons on every card; nitrogen needs a field soil test
  • No “Recommended”, no single best answer

Limits we show on screen

  • Weather is one NASA POWER point per county
  • Iowa crop calendar used for all 4 counties
  • No prices yet: revenue left empty, not guessed
  • SMAP / IMERG / NLDAS are coarse context only
  • MATLAB 2012 check: precipitation anomaly vs engine deficit, r = −0.87 (n = 4 counties; same POWER source, not independent)
  • V8 water demand vs engine deficit: in progress (different definitions)
  • Planned: 3D field simulation, economics, more counties
